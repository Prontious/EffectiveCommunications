# 8. The Commons of the Saints, and Lauds of the Dead

*Scan pp. 825–865 (Commons) and pp. 195–196 (Office of the Dead). The Commons open on p. 825 with
the rubric "Incipit commune sanctorum extra tempus paschale" ("Here begins the Common of the saints
outside Paschaltide"). The Paschaltide Common is given in the sanctorale at pp. 609–611 (see
`07-sanctorale.md`, at the end of April).*

Many sanctorale entries say "all as in the Common". The Lauds of each Common are set out below, so
those references can be completed. As everywhere in this book, the psalms are those of Sunday
Lauds: Ps 92 *Dominus regnavit*, Ps 99 *Jubilate*, Ps 62 *Deus Deus meus* [with Ps 66], the
canticle *Benedicite*, and Ps 148 *Laudate* [with 149–150]. When the *Oratio* is marked "qualis
evenerit" ("whichever applies"), the collect is that of the saint or the one given in the Common.
At the little Hours the antiphons of Lauds are used in order.

## The Vigil of an Apostle

*Scan p. 825.*

> *In vigilia unius apostoli vel plurimorum apostolorum non habentium proprietatem. Ad matutinum
> si fiat festum ix lectionum, vii lectio erit de evangelio sequenti; si autem fit de feria,
> leguntur tres sequentes lectiones et responsoria dominicalia secundum exigentiam temporis.*
>
> *On the vigil of one or several apostles that have no proper office. At Matins, if a feast of
> nine lessons is kept, the seventh lesson will be the following Gospel* [Jn 15:1, *Ego sum vitis
> vera*]; *if the office is of the feria, the three following lessons are read with the Sunday
> responsories as the season requires.*

**Collects**

> Quesumus omnipotens Deus, ut beatus N. apostolus tuus pro nobis imploret auxilium, ut a nostris
> reatibus absoluti, a cunctis etiam periculis eruamur.
>
> *We beseech thee, almighty God, that blessed N. thy apostle may implore help for us, that, set
> free from our sins, we may also be delivered from all dangers.*

> *Alia.* Concede quesumus omnipotens Deus, ut sicut apostolorum tuorum gloriosa natalitia
> prevenimus, sic ad tua beneficia promerenda maiestatem tuam pro nobis ipsi preveniant.
>
> *Another. Grant, we beseech thee, almighty God, that as we go before the glorious birthday of
> thy apostles, so they may go before thy majesty on our behalf to win thy benefits for us.*

## Common of Apostles

*Scan pp. 825–831. First Vespers chapter* Non vos me elegistis; *Matins invitatory* Regem
apostolorum Dominum venite adoremus; *hymn* Eterna Christi munera apostolorum gloriam.

**Collects for the feast** (p. 825):

> *Alia oratio.* Beatorum apostolorum tuorum N. et N., Domine, honore continuo plebs tua semper
> exultet, et his presulibus gubernetur, quorum doctrinis gaudet et meritis.
> *Alia.* Deus qui nos annua apostolorum tuorum N. et N. solennitate letificas: concede propitius,
> ut quorum gaudemus meritis instruamur exemplis.

> *Another collect. Let thy people, O Lord, ever rejoice in the continual honour of thy blessed
> apostles N. and N., and be governed by these leaders in whose teaching and merits it delights.*
> *Another. O God, who dost gladden us with the yearly feast of thy apostles N. and N.: mercifully
> grant that we may be taught by the example of those in whose merits we rejoice.*

**Lauds** (pp. 828–829)

**Latin**

> *In laudibus an.* Hoc est preceptum meum, ut diligatis invicem sicut dilexi vos. *ps.* Dominus
> regnavit.
>
> *an.* Maiorem caritatem nemo habet, ut animam suam ponat quis pro amicis suis. *ps.* Jubilate.
>
> *an.* Vos amici mei estis, si feceritis que precipio vobis, dicit Dominus. *ps.* Deus deus.
>
> *an.* Beati pacifici, beati mundo corde, quoniam ipsi Deum videbunt. *ps.* Benedicite.
>
> *an.* In patientia vestra possidebitis animas vestras. *ps.* Laudate.
>
> *Capitulum.* Per manus autem apostolorum fiebant signa et prodigia multa in plebe, et
> magnificabat eos populus. *℟.* Deo gratias.
>
> *Hymnus.*
> Exultet celum laudibus, / resultet terra gaudiis: / apostolorum gloriam / sacra canunt
> solennia.
> Vos secli iusti iudices / et vera mundi lumina, / votis precamur cordium: / audite preces
> supplicum.
> Qui celum verbo clauditis / serasque eius solvitis, / nos a peccatis omnibus / solvite iussu,
> quesumus.
> Quorum precepto subditur / salus et languor omnium, / sanate egros moribus, / nos reddentes
> virtutibus.
> Ut cum iudex advenerit / Christus in fine seculi, / nos sempiternis gaudiis / faciat esse
> compotes.
> Deo Patri sit gloria / eiusque soli Filio / cum Spiritu Paraclito / et nunc et in perpetuum.
> Amen.
> *℣.* Annunciaverunt opera Dei. *℟.* Et facta eius intellexerunt.
>
> *Ad Benedictus an.* Estote fortes in bello, et pugnate cum antiquo serpente, et accipietis regnum
> eternum, alleluia.
>
> *Oratio qualis evenerit.* (Terce: chapter *Non vos me elegistis*, ℟ *In omnem terram*; Sext:
> chapter *Iam non estis hospites et advene*, ℟ *Constitues eos*; None: chapter *Ibant apostoli
> gaudentes*, ℟ *Nimis honorati sunt*.)

**Translation**

> *At Lauds, antiphon:* This is my commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you.
> (Jn 15:12) *Psalm 92* The Lord hath reigned.
>
> *Antiphon:* Greater love than this no man hath, that a man lay down his life for his friends.
> (Jn 15:13) *Psalm 99* Make a joyful noise.
>
> *Antiphon:* You are my friends, if you do the things I command you, says the Lord. (Jn 15:14)
> *Psalm 62* O God, my God.
>
> *Antiphon:* Blessed are the peacemakers, blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God.
> (Mt 5:8–9) *Canticle* Bless [the Lord].
>
> *Antiphon:* In your patience you shall possess your souls. (Lk 21:19) *Psalm 148* Praise the
> Lord.
>
> *Chapter:* By the hands of the apostles were many signs and wonders wrought among the people,
> and the people magnified them. (Acts 5:12–13) *℟.* Thanks be to God.
>
> *Hymn.*
> Let heaven exult with praises, / let earth resound with joys: / the holy solemnities sing / the
> glory of the apostles.
> You just judges of the world / and true lights of the earth, / with the prayers of our hearts we
> beg: / hear the pleas of your suppliants.
> You who by a word shut heaven / and unloose its bars, / loose us from all our sins / at your
> command, we pray.
> You whose bidding governs / the health and sickness of all, / heal us who are sick in our ways, /
> restoring us to virtue.
> That when Christ the Judge / shall come at the end of the world, / he may make us sharers / of
> everlasting joys.
> To God the Father be glory / and to his only Son / with the Spirit the Paraclete / now and for
> ever. Amen.
> *℣.* They declared the works of God. *℟.* And understood his doings. (Ps 63:10)
>
> *Antiphon at the Benedictus:* Be strong in battle, and fight with the ancient serpent, and you
> shall receive the eternal kingdom, alleluia.
>
> *Collect, whichever applies.*

*Note.* For a military order the Benedictus antiphon *Estote fortes in bello* ("be strong in
battle") had an obvious resonance. The hymn *Exultet celum* is the one cued for most apostles'
feasts in the sanctorale. Its second stanza (*Vos secli iusti*) serves as a separate hymn for the
feast of the Patriarchs.

## Common of Evangelists

*Scan p. 831.*

> *In communi unius evangeliste antiphone, psalmi, responsoria, versus, capitula et cetera prout
> signantur in festis eorum.*
>
> *In the Common of one evangelist, the antiphons, psalms, responsories, versicles, chapters and
> the rest as they are marked on their feasts.*

That is, Lauds as on St Matthew (21 September): antiphons *Dilecti Deo et hominibus…*, chapter
*Cibavit illum*, Benedictus antiphon *In medio et in circuitu sedis*. See `07-sanctorale.md`.

## Common of One Martyr

*Scan pp. 831–836. First Vespers chapter* Beatus vir qui suffert tentationem; *hymn* Deus tuorum
militum; *Matins invitatory* Iustus florebit in domo Domini plantatus; gaudeamus et exultemus in
eius sacra solennitate.

**Collects** (p. 832)

> Presta quesumus omnipotens Deus, ut qui beati N. martyris tui natalicia colimus, intercessione
> eius in tui nominis amore roboremur.
> *Alia.* Adesto Domine supplicationibus nostris, ut qui ex iniquitate nostra reos nos esse
> cognoscimus, beati N. martyris tui intercessione liberemur.

> *Grant, we beseech thee, almighty God, that we who keep the heavenly birthday of blessed N. thy
> martyr may by his intercession be strengthened in the love of thy name.*
> *Another. Be present, O Lord, at our supplications, that we who know ourselves guilty through our
> iniquity may be set free by the intercession of blessed N. thy martyr.*

**Lauds** (pp. 834–835)

**Latin**

> *In laudibus an.* Qui me confessus fuerit coram hominibus, confitebor et ego eum coram Patre meo.
> *ps.* Dominus regnavit.
>
> *an.* Qui sequitur me non ambulat in tenebris, sed habebit lumen vite, dicit Dominus.
> *ps.* Jubilate.
>
> *an.* Si quis mihi ministraverit, honorificabit eum Pater meus qui est in celis, dicit Dominus.
> *ps.* Deus deus.
>
> *an.* Qui mihi ministrat me sequatur, et ubi ego sum illic sit et minister meus. *ps.* Benedicite.
>
> *an.* Volo, Pater, ut ubi ego sum illic sit et minister meus. *ps.* Laudate Dominum.
>
> *Capitulum.* Iste sanctus pro lege Dei sui certavit usque ad mortem, et a verbis impiorum non
> timuit; fundatus enim erat supra firmam petram.
>
> *Hymnus.*
> Martyr Dei, qui unicum / Patris sequendo Filium / victis triumphas hostibus, / victor fruens
> celestibus,
> Tui precatus munere / nostrum reatum dilue, / arcens mali contagium, / vite removens tedium.
> Soluta sunt iam vincula / tui sacrati corporis: / nos solve vinclis seculi / amore Filii Dei.
> Deo Patri sit gloria / eiusque soli Filio / cum Spiritu Paraclito / et nunc et in perpetuum.
> Amen.
> *℣.* Magna est gloria eius in salutari tuo. *℟.* Gloriam et magnum decorem impones super eum.
>
> *Ad Benedictus an.* Nisi granum frumenti cadens in terram mortuum fuerit, ipsum solum manet.
>
> *Oratio qualis evenerit. Ad horas an. de laudibus.* (Terce: chapter *Iustum deduxit Dominus*;
> Sext: *Beatus vir qui suffert*; None: *Iste sanctus pro lege*.)

**Translation**

> *At Lauds, antiphon:* Whoever shall confess me before men, I also will confess him before my
> Father. (Mt 10:32) *Psalm 92* The Lord hath reigned.
>
> *Antiphon:* He that followeth me walketh not in darkness, but shall have the light of life, says
> the Lord. (Jn 8:12) *Psalm 99* Make a joyful noise.
>
> *Antiphon:* If any man minister to me, my Father who is in heaven will honour him, says the Lord.
> (Jn 12:26) *Psalm 62* O God, my God.
>
> *Antiphon:* If any man minister to me, let him follow me; and where I am, there also shall my
> minister be. (Jn 12:26) *Canticle* Bless [the Lord].
>
> *Antiphon:* Father, I will that where I am, there also may my minister be. (cf. Jn 17:24)
> *Psalm 148* Praise the Lord.
>
> *Chapter:* This saint fought even unto death for the law of his God, and feared not the words of
> the wicked; for he was founded upon a firm rock.
>
> *Hymn.*
> Martyr of God, who, following / the only Son of the Father, / triumphest over conquered foes, /
> a victor enjoying heavenly things:
> By the gift of thy prayer / wash away our guilt, / keeping off the contagion of evil, / removing
> the weariness of life.
> Loosed now are the bonds / of thy holy body: / loose us from the chains of the world / for love
> of the Son of God.
> To God the Father be glory… Amen.
> *℣.* Great is his glory in thy salvation. *℟.* Glory and great beauty shalt thou lay upon him.
> (Ps 20:6)
>
> *Antiphon at the Benedictus:* Unless the grain of wheat falling into the ground die, itself
> remaineth alone. (Jn 12:24)
>
> *Collect, whichever applies. At the Hours, the antiphons of Lauds.*

*Vespers rubric* (p. 835): *Ad vesperas an. Qui me confessus, ps. Dixit Dominus, et cetera cum hac
sola, nisi fuerit duplex vel semiduplex, quia tunc dicuntur omnes antiphone de laudibus super
psalmos consuetos. Dominicis diebus loco psalmi Lauda Hierusalem dicitur psalmus In exitu.*
("At Vespers, the antiphon *Whoever shall confess me* with *Dixit Dominus* and the rest under this
one antiphon, unless the feast is a double or semidouble; then all the Lauds antiphons are said
over the usual psalms. On Sundays Ps 113 *In exitu* replaces *Lauda Hierusalem*.") This rule, that
on doubles and semidoubles the Lauds antiphons are repeated at Vespers, holds in all the Commons.

## Common of Many Martyrs

*Scan pp. 836–842. Matins invitatory* Regem regum Dominum venite adoremus, quia ipse est corona
sanctorum martyrum; *hymn* Eterna Christi munera et martyrum victorias.

**Lauds** (pp. 841–842)

**Latin**

> *In laudibus an.* Omnes sancti quanta passi sunt tormenta, ut securi pervenirent ad palmam
> martyrii. *ps.* Dominus regnavit.
>
> *an.* Cum palma ad regna pervenerunt sancti; coronas decoris meruerunt de manu Domini.
> *ps.* Jubilate.
>
> *an.* Corpora sanctorum in pace sepulta sunt, et vivent nomina eorum in eternum. *ps.* Deus deus.
>
> *an.* Martyres Domini, Dominum benedicite in eternum. *ps.* Benedicite.
>
> *an.* Exultabunt sancti in gloria, letabuntur in cubilibus suis. *ps.* Laudate Dominum.
>
> *Capitulum.* Reddet Deus mercedem laborum sanctorum suorum, et deducet eos in via mirabili, et
> fuit illis in velamento diei et in luce stellarum nocte.
>
> *Hymnus.*
> Rex gloriose martyrum, / corona confitentium, / qui respuentes terrena / perducis ad celestia,
> Aurem benignam protinus / appone nostris vocibus: / trophea sacra pangimus, / ignosce quod
> deliquimus.
> Tu vincis in martyribus / parcendo confessoribus: / tu vince nostra crimina / donando
> indulgentiam.

> *℣.* Mirabilis Deus in sanctis suis. *℟.* Et gloriosus in maiestate sua.

> celorum qui contempserunt vitam mundi*, ℟ *Letamini in Domino*; Sext: *Sancti et iusti in Domino
> gaudete*, ℟ *Exultent iusti*; None: *Fulgebunt iusti*, ℟ *Iusti autem in perpetuum vivent*.
> Vespers: antiphon *Omnes sancti*; chapter *Sancti ludibria et verbera experti*; hymn *Sanctorum
> meritis*; Magnificat antiphon *Gaudent in celis anime sanctorum*.)

**Translation**

> *At Lauds, antiphon:* How great the torments all the saints suffered, that they might come
> safely to the palm of martyrdom. *Psalm 92* The Lord hath reigned.
>
> *Antiphon:* With palms the saints came to the kingdom; they won crowns of beauty from the hand of
> the Lord. *Psalm 99* Make a joyful noise.
>
> *Antiphon:* The bodies of the saints are buried in peace, and their names shall live for ever.

>
> *Antiphon:* Ye martyrs of the Lord, bless the Lord for ever. *Canticle* Bless [the Lord].
>
> *Antiphon:* The saints shall rejoice in glory; they shall be joyful in their beds. (Ps 149:5)
> *Psalm 148* Praise the Lord.
>
> *Chapter:* God will render to his saints the reward of their labours, and will lead them by a
> marvellous way; and he was to them for a covering by day and for the light of stars by night.
> (Wis 10:17)
>
> *Hymn.*
> O glorious King of martyrs, / crown of those who confess thee, / who leadest to heavenly things /
> those who spurn the earthly,
> Lend at once a kindly ear / to our voices: / we sing of sacred trophies; / forgive what we have
> done amiss.
> Thou conquerest in the martyrs, / sparing the confessors: / conquer our sins / by granting
> pardon.
> To God the Father be glory… Amen.
> *℣.* God is wonderful in his saints. *℟.* And glorious in his majesty.
>
> *Antiphon at the Benedictus:* The just shall shine, and shall run to and fro like sparks among the
> reeds; they shall judge nations, and shall reign for ever. (Wis 3:7–8)
>
> *Collect as the case may be. At the Hours, the antiphons of Lauds.*

## Common of One Confessor not a Bishop (and of an Abbot)

*Scan pp. 848–851. First Vespers chapter* Iustum deduxit Dominus; *Magnificat antiphon* Similabo
eum viro sapienti qui edificavit domum suam supra petram.

**Collects** (p. 849)

> Concede quesumus omnipotens Deus, ut ad meliorem vitam beati N. confessoris tui exempla nos
> provocent, quatenus cuius solennia agimus, etiam actus imitemur.
> *[Pro abbate.]* Intercessio nos quesumus Domine beati N. abbatis commendet, ut quod nostris
> meritis non valemus, eius patrocinio assequamur.

> *Grant, we beseech thee, almighty God, that the example of blessed N. thy confessor may stir us
> to a better life, so that we may also imitate the deeds of him whose feast we keep.*
> *[For an abbot.] May the intercession of blessed N. the abbot commend us, we beseech thee, O
> Lord, that what we cannot attain by our own merits we may obtain by his patronage.*

*Invitatory for an abbot:* Unum Deum in Trinitate fideliter adoremus, cuius fide Deo vivit sanctus
abbas N. ("Let us faithfully adore one God in Trinity, by faith in whom the holy abbot N. lives to
God"). *Otherwise:* Iustus florebit.

**Lauds** (p. 851)

> *In laudibus an.* Iustum deduxit Dominus per vias rectas, et ostendit illi regnum Dei.
> *ps.* Dominus regnavit.

> vigilantem. *ps.* Jubilate.

> communi unius confessoris et episcopi. ℣.* Iustus germinabit.

## The end of the book

*Scan pp. 866–871.* After the Commons the book gives occasional rites. None belongs to Lauds, but
they bear on the sanctorale notes and on the book's setting:

- the Sunday blessing of salt and water (p. 866), and the blessing of ashes for Ash Wednesday
  (p. 868);
- blessings of a house, of bread, and the rite for the **reception of the habit of the Order**
  (p. 869);
- the **form of absolution used on the feast of St Pantaleon "on account of the indulgences of
  that day"** (p. 870). This explains why Pantaleon (27 July) is kept as a double;
- the **prayers against the pagans** (*preces contra paganos*), said daily at the morning Mass
  after the offertory, kneeling before the altar (pp. 870–871). They include *℣. Domine salvum fac
  magistrum nostrum cum toto exercitu suo* ("O Lord, save our Master with all his army") and
  invoke SS John the Baptist, John the Evangelist and George. The collect asks God to "illumine
  thy servant N. our Master, our Order and its army … and help us against the enemies of the
  cross";
- a collect for the **Friday procession round the church "on account of the earthquake"**
  (*propter terremotum*, p. 871). It asks God to "bless us and this island with thy glorious
  blessing, that earthquake and all adversity may depart from it". Rhodes suffered a
  catastrophic earthquake in 1481.

**Colophon** (p. 871):

> Explicit breviarium secundum usum ordinis sancti Iohannis Hierosolymitani ex approbato usu
> dominici sepulchri excerptum, cum multis non vulgaribus officiis. Quod plerisque quibus
> squalebat mendis prius castigatum impressit Lugduni Cyriacus Hochperg, eiusdem civitatis et
> bibliopola et calcographus non mediocris. Anno post virgineum partum decimoseptimo supra mille et
> quingentesimum, ad decimumoctavum kal. Ianuarias.
>
> *Here ends the Breviary according to the use of the Order of St John of Jerusalem, drawn from the
> approved use of the Lord's Sepulchre, with many uncommon offices. First corrected of the many
> errors that disfigured it, it was printed at Lyon by Cyriacus Hochperg, bookseller and printer of
> that city, and no mean one, in the year 1517 after the virgin birth, on the eighteenth day before
> the Kalends of January* [15 December 1517].

The colophon confirms in print what the rubrics showed throughout: this Hospitaller breviary is
"drawn from the approved use of the Lord's Sepulchre". That explains the Jerusalem bishops, the
Patriarchs, the Commemoration of the Resurrection, and the other "uncommon offices".

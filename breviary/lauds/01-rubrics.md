# 1. Rubrics — the general order of Lauds

The breviary opens (after the calendar) with an *Ordinarium*: "the ordinal of the
canonical hours of the brethren of the Hospital of the sacred order of St John of
Jerusalem, excerpted from the approved use of the Lord's Sepulchre". Only passages that
govern Lauds are given here.

---

## Rubric 1 — Lauds on ferial days (scan pp. 21–22)

**Latin**

> *Qui facit officium, finito ultimo responsorio, dicit versiculum, [quem dicit] sacerdos.
> Deinde dicuntur* Laudes, *et incipiuntur sic:* Deus in adiutorium. *Et prosequitur ut
> supra. Postea dicuntur antiphone et psalmi prout in psalterio signatur. Deinde
> capitulum dicitur semper, excepto in triduo ante pascha et per ebdomadam pasche.
> Hymnus et versiculus deinceps dicuntur prout in psalterio et aliis locis signatur;
> quibus dictis dicitur antiphona et psalmus* Benedictus. *Postea dicitur* Kyrieleyson
> *ter,* Christeleyson *ter,* Kyrieleyson *ter, et preces cum* Pater noster, *prout tempus
> requirit et ut infra signatur suo loco. Postea dicitur oratio ut contigerit illo die.*
>
> *Et nota quod ferialibus diebus tempore paschali, videlicet a pascha usque ad
> penthecosten, non dicuntur preces, sed immediate oratio dicitur [post]* Dominus
> vobiscum. *Et etiam non dicuntur preces in aliquibus diebus ferialibus, ut videbitur
> suis locis. Finita oratione et versiculo* Benedicamus, *dicuntur suffragia sanctorum
> prout tempus requirit, et dicitur post ultimam orationem versiculus* Benedicamus;
> *excepto a dominica de passione usque post octavam pasche. Et nota quod licet
> suffragia non dicantur, tamen semper debent fieri memorie de sancto Iohanne baptista
> post matutinas et vesperas beate Marie virginis. Et etiam non dicuntur suffragia in
> aliquibus vigiliis, ut videbitur. [Et] finiuntur matutine sic.*
>
> *Nota quod a feria iiii die cinerum usque ad triduum ante pascha, finitis matutinis,
> post suffragia dicuntur septem psalmi penitentiales cum* Gloria patri *ad quemlibet
> psalmum, et dicitur sola antiphona* Ne reminiscaris; *postea letania, psalmi tres,
> preces et orationes sequentes, ut continentur suo loco.*

**Translation**

> The one who leads the office, when the last responsory [of Matins] is finished, says
> the versicle, which the priest says. Then **Lauds** are said, and they begin thus:
> *O God, come to my assistance* (Deus in adiutorium), and it continues as above.
> After this the antiphons and psalms are said as marked in the psalter. Then the
> **chapter** is always said, except in the Triduum before Easter and through Easter
> week. The **hymn and versicle** are then said as marked in the psalter and in other
> places; and when these are said, the antiphon and the psalm [i.e. canticle]
> **Benedictus** are said. After this is said *Kyrie eleison* three times, *Christe
> eleison* three times, *Kyrie eleison* three times, and the **preces** with the *Pater
> noster*, as the season requires and as is marked below in its place. After this the
> **collect** is said, whichever falls on that day.
>
> And note that on ferial days in Eastertide — that is, from Easter to Pentecost — the
> preces are not said, but the collect is said immediately after *Dominus vobiscum*.
> The preces are also omitted on certain other ferial days, as will be seen in their
> places. When the collect and the versicle *Benedicamus [Domino]* are finished, the
> **suffrages of the saints** are said as the season requires, and the versicle
> *Benedicamus* is said after the last collect — except from Passion Sunday until after
> the octave of Easter. And note that even when the suffrages are not said, a
> **memorial of St John the Baptist** must always be made after Matins [i.e. Lauds] and
> Vespers of the Blessed Virgin Mary. The suffrages are also not said on certain vigils,
> as will be seen. And so Matins [with Lauds] end.
>
> Note that from the Wednesday of Ash Wednesday until the Triduum before Easter, when
> Matins [and Lauds] are finished, after the suffrages the **seven penitential psalms**
> are said with *Gloria Patri* after each psalm and with the single antiphon *Ne
> reminiscaris* ("Remember not, Lord, our offences"); then the litany, three psalms,
> and the preces and collects that follow, as they are given in their place.

*Note.* "Matins" (*matutine*) in this book often means the whole night-and-dawn office,
of which Lauds is the closing part; here it plainly includes Lauds.

## Rubric 8 — Lauds on feasts of nine lessons (scan pp. 25–26)

**Latin**

> In laudibus *dicuntur v. antiphone super v. psalmos, scilicet* Dominus regnavit decorem
> indutus *etc. Capitulum, hymnus, versiculus, antiphona super psalmum* Benedictus, *et
> oratio que contigerit in ipsis festis. Post matutinas, si festum sit in quadragesima vel
> in adventu, semper fit memoria de dictis temporibus. Etiam si fiat in aliqua dominica,
> fiat memoria de ipsa, et etiam in paschali tempore.*
>
> … *Ad primam, tertiam, sextam et nonam dicuntur antiphone de laudibus, cum [capitulo,]
> responsorio [et] versiculo [?] ut contigerit in ipsis festis. Oratio que dicitur ad
> matutinas dicitur etiam ad tertiam, sextam et nonam et vesperas. Ad vesperas sola
> antiphona dicitur que est prima de laudibus, et dicuntur quinque psalmi supradicti sub
> sola antiphona, nisi in crastinum fit festum semiduplex vel duplex, quia tunc omnes
> antiphone dicuntur, ut videbitur.*

**Translation**

> At **Lauds** five antiphons are said over the five psalms — that is, *The Lord hath
> reigned, he is clothed with beauty* (Ps 92) and the rest; [then] the chapter, the hymn,
> the versicle, the antiphon on the psalm *Benedictus*, and the collect proper to the
> feast. After Matins [and Lauds], if the feast falls in Lent or Advent, a **memorial of
> the season** is always made. Likewise if it falls on a Sunday, a memorial of the Sunday
> is made — and in Eastertide too.
>
> … At Prime, Terce, Sext and None the **antiphons from Lauds** are said, with the
> chapter, responsory and versicle proper to the feast. The collect said at Matins is also
> said at Terce, Sext, None and Vespers. At Vespers only one antiphon is said — **the first
> antiphon of Lauds** — and the five psalms above are said under that single antiphon,
> unless a semidouble or double feast falls on the next day, for then all the antiphons
> are said, as will be seen.

*Note.* The five "psalms" of festal Lauds in this use are those of Sunday Lauds:
Ps 92 *Dominus regnavit*, Ps 99 *Jubilate*, Ps 62 *Deus deus meus* (with Ps 66),
the canticle *Benedicite*, and Ps 148 *Laudate Dominum de celis* (with Ps 149–150).
This is confirmed against the psalter below.

## Rubric 10 — Lauds on semidouble feasts (scan p. 27)

**Latin**

> *Ad matutinas dicitur more festi novem lectionum; et etiam [in]* laudibus *antiphona ad*
> Benedictus *semper dicitur ante psalmum et iteratur post psalmum. Ad primam dicuntur
> psalmi ut continentur in psalterio. Antiphone de laudibus ad horas. Post capitulum
> dicuntur responsoria brevia cum duplici* alleluya, *et versiculus sequens cum simplici*
> alleluya. …
>
> *Ad secundas vesperas antiphone de laudibus dicuntur super psalmos, prout requirit in
> festo ipso.*

**Translation**

> Matins is said in the manner of a feast of nine lessons; and at **Lauds** the antiphon
> to the *Benedictus* is always said [in full] before the canticle and repeated after
> it. At Prime the psalms are said as they stand in the psalter. The **antiphons of Lauds**
> are used at the [little] hours. After the chapter the short responsories are said with a
> double *alleluia*, and the following versicle with a single *alleluia*. …
>
> At second Vespers the **antiphons of Lauds** are said over the psalms, as is required on
> the feast itself.

## Rubric 11 — Lauds on double feasts (scan p. 28)

The rubric first describes how the Magnificat antiphon is sung at first Vespers of a
double, and then applies the same pattern to the Benedictus at Lauds.

**Latin**

> *[Ad primas vesperas] … Ad psalmum* Magnificat *antiphona dicitur tota ante psalmum;
> postea dicitur psalmus; quo dicto, antequam dicatur* Gloria patri, *iteratur ipsa
> antiphona, et postea dicitur* Gloria patri *etc., et reiteratur antiphona.* …
>
> In laudibus *omnes antiphone duplicantur in modum supradictum in nocturnis. Capitulum,
> hymnus ut supra, versiculus ut supra. Ad* Benedictus *antiphona dicitur ter, ut in modum
> vesperarum. Ad psalmum* Magnificat [*similiter*]. Benedicamus *semper dicitur ad
> matutinas, vesperas et completorium cum duplici* alleluya. … *Antiphone de laudibus
> dicuntur semper ad horas, nisi sint alie proprie.* …
>
> *Ad secundas vesperas antiphone de laudibus, nisi sint alie proprie, et dicuntur in modum
> supradictum in primis.*

**Translation**

> [At first Vespers] … At the psalm *Magnificat* the antiphon is said in full before the
> canticle; then the canticle is said; and when it is finished, before the *Gloria Patri*
> is said, the antiphon is repeated; then the *Gloria Patri* etc. is said, and the
> antiphon is repeated again. …
>
> At **Lauds** all the antiphons are **doubled** (sung in full before and after their
> psalm) in the manner described above for the nocturns. The chapter and hymn as above;
> the versicle as above. At the **Benedictus** the antiphon is said **three times**, in the
> manner of [the Magnificat at] Vespers. *Benedicamus [Domino]* is always said at Matins
> [with Lauds], Vespers and Compline with a double *alleluia*. … The **antiphons of Lauds**
> are always said at the [little] hours, unless they have other proper ones. …
>
> At second Vespers the **antiphons of Lauds** [are used], unless there are other proper
> ones, and they are said in the manner described above for first Vespers.

## Rubric 12 — Lauds within solemn octaves (scan pp. 28–29)

**Latin**

> *Circa octavas que solemnes dicuntur sic est fiendum.* … *Omnes versiculi per octavam,
> tam ad matutinas quam ad* laudes *et ad omnes horas, semper dicuntur cum uno* alleluya,
> *et etiam versiculus sacerdotalis.* …
>
> In laudibus *antiphona sola dicitur super v. psalmos. Capitulum, hymnus, versiculus
> prout in festo requirit. Ad* Benedictus *antiphona dicitur una de nocturnis, incipiendo ad
> secundam, et sic consecutive dicuntur ad* Benedictus *et ad* Magnificat. *Oratio semper
> dicitur illa que dicitur in festo ad matutinas.* Benedicamus *ad matutinas, vesperas et
> completorium cum duplici* alleluya. *Si fiat memoria, versiculus dicitur cum uno*
> alleluya, *et etiam* Benedicamus *cum uno* alleluya. *Ad primam antiphone de laudibus;
> non dicitur psalmus* Quicunque. …
>
> *Dominica infra octavas* … *Septima lectio de evangelio dominice, cetera de octava.* In
> laudibus *sola antiphona dicitur super psalmos. Capitulum et cetera ut in die, cum
> memoria de dominica. Ad horas ut in die, cum oratione que dicitur ad matutinas.*

**Translation**

> Concerning the octaves called solemn, this is to be done. … Throughout the octave all
> versicles — at Matins, at **Lauds** and at every hour — are always said with a single
> *alleluia*, and so is the priest's versicle. …
>
> At **Lauds** a **single antiphon** is said over the five psalms. The chapter, hymn and
> versicle as the feast requires. At the **Benedictus** one of the **antiphons of the
> nocturns** is said, beginning with the second, and so they are taken in turn for the
> *Benedictus* and the *Magnificat* [on the successive days of the octave]. The collect is
> always the one said at Matins on the feast. *Benedicamus* at Matins, Vespers and Compline
> with a double *alleluia*; if a memorial is made, its versicle is said with one
> *alleluia*, and the *Benedicamus* also with one *alleluia*. At Prime the **antiphons of
> Lauds** [are used]; the psalm *Quicunque* is not said. …
>
> **The Sunday within an octave:** … The seventh lesson is from the Sunday gospel, the rest
> of the octave. At **Lauds** a single antiphon is said over the psalms; the chapter and
> the rest as on the feast day, with a **memorial of the Sunday**. At the hours as on the
> feast day, with the collect said at Matins.

**The octave day itself (end of Rubric 12, scan p. 30)**

> *In octava omnia ut in dominica, sed in* laudibus *omnes antiphone dicuntur, et oratio
> propria si sit, que dicetur ad horas. Et finiuntur octave post completorium.*

> On the **octave day** all is as on the Sunday [within the octave], except that at
> **Lauds all the antiphons** are said, and the proper collect if there is one, which is
> also said at the hours. And the octave ends after Compline.

## Rubric 13 — Lauds within common (non-solemn) octaves (scan p. 30)

**Latin**

> *Circa octavas que dicuntur communes sic est fiendum. Ad matutinas officium beate Marie
> dicitur prout requirit suo tempore.* … In laudibus *antiphona prima supra quinque
> psalmos. Capitulum, hymnus, versiculus ut in die. Ad* Benedictus *antiphona de antiphonis
> nocturnorum, ut supra dictum est, nisi alie proprie signentur. Oratio prout requirit. Ad
> omnes horas ut in festis ix. lectionum, addito quod responsoria brevia dicuntur cum
> duplici* alleluya, *et versiculi horarum cum simplici* alleluya. *Ad* Benedicamus *non
> dicitur* alleluya. …
>
> *Dominica infra octavas, ut supra in precedenti rubrica* … In laudibus *sola antiphona
> dicitur; cetera ut [in festo, et] memoria de dominica. In octava omnia ut in dominica
> infra octavas, ix. lectiones.* In laudibus *omnes antiphone dicuntur; cetera ut supra.
> Ad vesperas prima antiphona de laudibus super psalmos quinque.* …
>
> *Notandum est quod quandocumque festum novem lectionum vel octave communes celebrantur
> diebus sabbatis, si dominica sequens proxima incipiatur historia aliqua, sicut* Domine ne
> in ira, *vel de dominicis quadragesime vel etiam adventus, et prima dominica post octavas
> corporis Christi, tunc vespere integre erunt de dominica, psalmus* Benedictus *et
> antiphona ut in sabbatis, et memoria de sancto vel de octava.*

**Translation**

> Concerning the octaves called **common**, this is to be done. At Matins the office of the
> Blessed Mary is said as its season requires. … At **Lauds** the **first antiphon** [of
> the feast] over all five psalms. Chapter, hymn and versicle as on the feast day. At the
> **Benedictus**, an antiphon taken from the **antiphons of the nocturns**, as said above,
> unless other proper ones are marked. The collect as required. At all the hours as on
> feasts of nine lessons, except that the short responsories are said with a double
> *alleluia* and the versicles of the hours with a single *alleluia*. No *alleluia* is
> added to *Benedicamus*. …
>
> **The Sunday within** [a common octave], as in the preceding rubric … At **Lauds** a
> single antiphon is said; the rest as on the feast, with a memorial of the Sunday. **On
> the octave day** all as on the Sunday within the octave, with nine lessons. At **Lauds
> all the antiphons** are said; the rest as above. At Vespers the **first antiphon of
> Lauds** over the five psalms. …
>
> Note that whenever a feast of nine lessons or a common octave is kept on a **Saturday**,
> and on the following Sunday some *historia* [cycle of Matins readings and chants]
> begins — such as *Domine ne in ira*, or those of the Sundays of Lent or Advent, or the
> first Sunday after the octave of Corpus Christi — then Vespers are wholly of the Sunday;
> the canticle **Benedictus** [at that Saturday's Lauds?] with its antiphon as on
> Saturdays; and a memorial of the saint or of the octave.

*Note.* The last sentence is compressed in the original; the reference to the
*Benedictus* "as on Saturdays" most likely means the Saturday Lauds keeps the ferial
Saturday Benedictus antiphon.

## Rubric 15 — Lauds in the Advent tables (scan pp. 31–40)

Rubric 15 (*de ordinatione officiorum in adventu secundum diversa tempora*) is a set of
seven tables, one for each weekday on which Christmas can fall. Each gives a day-by-day
plan for Advent. It is mostly about which saint's feast displaces which feria, but it
repeatedly prescribes **which Lauds antiphon and which Benedictus antiphon** to use. It
is summarised here rather than transcribed in full, since the antiphon texts themselves
appear in the Advent part of the breviary.

### The opening formula of each table

> *In anno quo nativitas domini [feria …] evenerit, tunc omnes antiphone de laudibus
> [dominice], scilicet* Regnavit dominus, *pronunciantur in [die …]; et sunt in adventu
> [tot] septimane et [tot] dies. Commemoratio resurrectionis dominice hoc anno agetur
> [die …].*

> In a year when Christmas falls on [a given weekday], all the **Lauds antiphons [of the
> Sunday], namely *Regnavit Dominus*, are "pronounced"** on [date]; and Advent has
> [so many] weeks and days. The **Commemoration of the Lord's Resurrection** is kept this
> year on [date].

| Christmas falls on | *Regnavit Dominus* "pronounced" | Length of Advent | Commemoration of the Resurrection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sunday (p. 31) | 30 Oct (*penultimo die octobris*) | 4 full weeks | 20 Nov (12 kal. Dec.) |
| Monday (p. 33) | 12 Nov (day after St Martin) | 3 weeks + 1 day | 26 Nov (6 kal. Dec., St Linus) |
| Tuesday (p. 34) | 14 Oct (St Callixtus) | 3 weeks + 2 days | 25 Nov (7 kal. Dec., St Catherine) |
| Wednesday (p. 35) | 27 Oct (vigil of SS Simon & Jude) | 3 weeks + 3 days | 24 Nov (8 kal. Dec., St Chrysogonus) |
| Thursday (p. 37) | 9 Nov (St Theodore) | 3 weeks + 4 days | 23 Nov (9 kal. Dec., St Clement) |
| Friday (p. 38) | 25 Oct (SS Crispin & Crispinian) | 3 weeks + 5 days | 22 Nov (10 kal. Dec., St Cecilia) |
| Saturday (p. 39) | 31 Oct (St Quentin) | 4 weeks less 1 day | 21 Nov (11 kal. Dec., Presentation of Our Lady) |

*Notes.* I checked every date against a perpetual calendar: in each year type both dates
fall on a **Sunday**, and the Commemoration of the Resurrection — a feast proper to the
Holy Sepulchre rite — is always **the Sunday before Advent**. The rubric does not say why
the Sunday Lauds antiphons beginning *Regnavit Dominus* (Ps 92) are "pronounced" on the
earlier Sunday. It may mean they are announced or resumed then, before the last Sundays
of the year, but that is my inference. The Monday-year numeral is hard to read; the
date follows from "the day after St Martin" and the Sunday check.

### The logic of the Lauds cues

Late Advent has a fixed set of **ferial Lauds antiphons**, each tied to a weekday:
Monday *Ecce veniet*, Tuesday *Rorate celi*, Wednesday *Prophete*, Thursday *De Syon*,
Friday *Constantes*, Saturday *Intuemini*, each paired with its own Benedictus antiphon.
When a saint's feast — above all **St Lazarus (17 Dec)** or **St Thomas the Apostle
(21 Dec)** — would displace one of them, the rubric moves that weekday's Lauds to a free
feria earlier in Advent, "because of the feast of St Lazarus/Thomas" (*propter festum
sancti Lazari/Thome*). The Lauds antiphons are then also used at the little hours
(*ad horas antiphone de laudibus cum oratione dominicali*: "at the hours, the Lauds
antiphons with the Sunday collect").

### A full Saturday Lauds (scan p. 34)

In the Monday-Christmas year, Advent IV Sunday is Christmas Eve, so its Lauds antiphons
are sung on the Ember Saturday with that day's own psalms. This entry also shows the
**psalm scheme of Saturday Lauds** in this rite:

> *In laudibus antiphona* Canite tuba, *que est sequentis dominice; psalmus* Miserere.
> *Antiphona* Ecce veniet; *psalmus* Bonum est. *Antiphona* Erunt prava; *psalmus* Deus
> deus. *Antiphona* Expectetur; *psalmus* Audite celi. *Antiphona* Omnipotens; *psalmus*
> Laudate. *Ad* Benedictus *antiphona* Quomodo fiet istud. *Oratio* Deus qui conspicis,
> *que dicetur ad horas cum antiphonis de laudibus.*

> At Lauds, the antiphon *Blow the trumpet [in Sion]* (*Canite tuba*), which belongs to
> the following Sunday, with Psalm 50 *Have mercy on me, O God*; the antiphon *Behold, he
> will come* (*Ecce veniet*), with Psalm 91 *It is good to give praise*; the antiphon *The
> crooked shall be [made straight]* (*Erunt prava*), with Psalm 62 *O God, my God*; the
> antiphon *Let [the word of the Lord] be awaited* (*Expectetur*), with the canticle
> *Hear, O heavens* (Deut 32); the antiphon *Thy almighty [word]* (*Omnipotens*), with
> Psalm 148 *Praise the Lord* [and Pss 149–150]. At the *Benedictus*, the antiphon *How
> shall this be done?* (*Quomodo fiet istud*). The collect *O God, who seest*, which is
> also said at the hours with the Lauds antiphons.

### Antiphon incipits used above (translated)

*Antequam convenirent* "Before they came together" · *Beatam me dicent* "[All
generations] shall call me blessed" · *Benedicta tu* "Blessed art thou" · *Bethleem*

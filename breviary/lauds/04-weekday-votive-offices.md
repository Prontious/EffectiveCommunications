# 4. Weekday votive offices — Lauds (St John the Baptist, Holy Cross, Our Lady)

After the psalter the book prints three short offices for weekdays left free of feasts
(*diebus vacantibus*) between the octave of Corpus Christi and Advent: St John the
Baptist — the Order's patron — from Monday to Thursday, the Holy Cross on Friday, and Our
Lady on Saturday. Each is kept "as a feast of nine lessons", and so has a full festal
Lauds.

---

## The opening rubric (scan p. 159)

**Latin**

> *Honores sancti Iohannis baptiste.*
> *Sequuntur officia sancti Iohannis baptiste, sancte crucis et beate Marie, dicenda super
> ebdomadam diebus vacantibus ab octava corporis Christi usque ad adventum, ut videbitur.
> Et primo sequitur officium sancti Iohannis baptiste, celebrandum diebus vacantibus super
> ebdomadam, exceptis diebus veneris et sabbati, a supradicto tempore. Ad primas vesperas
> antiphone et psalmi ut contigerit illo die. Et fit ut in festis ix lectionum, tam sancti
> Iohannis baptiste quam sancte crucis et beate Marie virginis.*

**Translation**

> *The honours of St John the Baptist.*
> There follow the offices of St John the Baptist, of the Holy Cross and of the Blessed
> Mary, to be said through the week on free days from the octave of Corpus Christi until
> Advent, as will be seen. First comes the office of St John the Baptist, to be kept on
> the free weekdays of that season except Fridays and Saturdays. At first Vespers, the
> antiphons and psalms as they fall that day. And it is kept as a feast of nine lessons —
> for St John the Baptist, for the Holy Cross and for the Blessed Virgin Mary alike.

---

## Lauds of St John the Baptist (scan p. 164)

**Latin**

> In laudibus. *Antiphona.* Elisabeth Zacharie magnum virum genuit, Iohannem baptistam,
> precursorem Domini. *Psalmus* Dominus regnavit.
>
> *Antiphona.* Innuebant patri eius quem vellet vocari eum; et scripsit dicens: Iohannes
> est nomen eius. *Psalmus* Iubilate Deo.
>
> *Antiphona.* Iohannes vocabitur nomen eius, et in nativitate eius multi gaudebunt.
> *Psalmus* Deus deus meus.
>
> *Antiphona.* Iohannes vocabitur nomen eius; vinum et siceram non bibet, et multi in
> nativitate eius gaudebunt. *Psalmus* Benedicite omnia.
>
> *Antiphona.* Inter natos mulierum non surrexit maior Iohanne baptista. *Psalmus*
> Laudate Dominum.
>
> *Capitulum.* Audite, insule, et attendite, populi de longe: Dominus ab utero vocavit me;
> de ventre matris mee recordatus est nominis mei. ℟. Deo gratias.
>
> *Hymnus.* O nimis felix meritique celsi, / nesciens labem nivei pudoris, / prepotens
> martyr heremique cultor, / maxime vatum.
> Serta ter denis alios coronant / aucta crementis, duplicata quosdam; / trina centeno
> cumulata fructu / te, sacer, ornant.
> Nunc potens nostri meritis opimis / pectoris duros lapides repelle, / asperum planans
> iter, et reflexos / dirige calles;
> Ut pius mundi sator et redemptor, / mentibus pulsa livione puris, / rite dignetur
> veniens sacratos / ponere gressus.
> Laudibus cives celebrant superni / te, Deus simplex pariterque trine; / supplices et nos
> veniam precamur: / parce redemptis. Amen.
>
> ℣. Magna est gloria [eius in salutari tuo].
>
> *Ad* Benedictus *antiphona.* Apertum est os Zacharie, et prophetavit dicens: Benedictus
> Deus Israel.
>
> *Oratio ut supra:* Presta, quesumus, omnipotens Deus, ut familia tua per viam salutis
> incedat, et beati Iohannis precursoris Christi hortamenta sectando, ad eum quem predixit
> secura perveniat, Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum filium tuum. Qui tecum vivit et regnat
> in unitate [Spiritus sancti Deus …]. *Ad horas antiphone de laudibus.*

**Translation**

> **At Lauds.** *Antiphon:* Elizabeth, wife of Zachary, bore a great man, John the
> Baptist, the forerunner of the Lord. *Psalm 92* The Lord hath reigned.
>
> *Antiphon:* They made signs to his father, how he would have him called; and he wrote,
> saying: John is his name. *Psalm 99* Shout with joy to God.
>
> *Antiphon:* His name shall be called John, and many shall rejoice at his birth.
> *Psalm 62* O God, my God.
>
> *Antiphon:* His name shall be called John; he shall drink no wine nor strong drink, and
> many shall rejoice at his birth. *Canticle* Bless [the Lord], all [ye works].
>
> *Antiphon:* Among those born of women there has not risen a greater than John the
> Baptist. *Psalm 148* Praise the Lord.
>
> *Chapter:* Listen, O islands, and hearken, ye peoples from afar: the Lord has called me
> from the womb; from my mother's body he has remembered my name. (Is 49:1) ℟. Thanks be
> to God.
>
> *Hymn (Paul the Deacon, from* Ut queant laxis*):*
> O blessed beyond measure, of lofty merit, knowing no stain on your snow-white purity,
> mighty martyr and dweller in the desert, greatest of prophets!
> Garlands, increased thirtyfold, crown some; doubled, crown others; but heaped with a
> hundredfold fruit, three crowns adorn you, holy one.
> Now, mighty by your rich merits, cast out the hard stones from our hearts, levelling the
> rough road and straightening the winding paths,
> That the loving Maker and Redeemer of the world, finding our minds pure and envy
> driven out, may duly deign to come and set his holy steps there.
> The citizens of heaven celebrate you with praises, O God, one and three; we too, as
> suppliants, beg pardon: spare those you have redeemed. Amen.
>
> ℣. Great is [his] glory [in thy salvation]. (Ps 20:6)
>
> *Antiphon at the Benedictus:* The mouth of Zachary was opened, and he prophesied,
> saying: Blessed be the God of Israel.
>
> *The collect as above* (at first Vespers): Grant, we beseech thee, almighty God, that
> thy household may walk in the way of salvation, and, by following the exhortations of
> blessed John, the forerunner of Christ, may come safely to him whom John foretold, our
> Lord Jesus Christ, thy Son: who lives and reigns with thee in the unity [of the Holy
> Spirit, God …]. *At the [little] hours, the antiphons of Lauds.*

*Notes.* At Lauds the book cues the collect only by *Presta quesumus*; its full text is
printed at first Vespers (scan pp. 159–160). The hymn's middle stanzas come from Paul the Deacon's hymn for the Nativity
of St John (*Ut queant laxis*), whose first stanza this office uses at Vespers.

## Lauds of the Holy Cross — Fridays (scan p. 179; collect and hymn from p. 176)

**Latin**

> *[End of Matins:]* Te Deum. *Sacerdotalis* ℣. Hoc signum crucis erit in celo. ℟. Cum
> Dominus ad iudicandum venerit.
>
> In laudibus. *Antiphona.* O magnum pietatis opus: mors mortua tunc est, quando in ligno
> mortua vita fuit. *Psalmus* Dominus regnavit.
>
> *Antiphona.* Salva nos, Christe salvator, per virtutem sancte crucis; qui salvasti
> Petrum in mari, miserere nobis. *Psalmus* Iubilate.
>
> *Antiphona.* Per signum crucis de inimicis nostris libera nos, Deus noster. *Psalmus*
> Deus deus meus.
>
> *Antiphona.* Crux benedicta nitet, Dominus qua carne pependit, atque cruore suo vulnera
> nostra lavit. *Psalmus* Benedicite.
>
> *Antiphona.* Ecce crucem Domini: fugite, partes adverse; vicit leo de tribu Iuda, radix
> David, alleluia. *Psalmus* Laudate Dominum de celis.
>
> *Capitulum.* Mihi autem absit gloriari nisi in cruce Domini nostri Iesu Christi, per
> quem mihi mundus crucifixus est, et ego mundo. ℟. Deo gratias.
>
> *Hymnus [the last three stanzas of* Signum crucis mirabile*, cued]:*
> Tu, rex Christe piissime, / huius crucis signaculo / horis, momentis omnibus /
> munire nos non abnuas;
> Ut ore tibi consono / et corde devotissimo / possimus omni tempore / laudes referre
> debitas.
> Gloria tibi, Domine, / qui nos salvasti in cruce, / cum patre et sancto spiritu / in
> sempiterna secula. Amen.
>
> ℣. Omnis terra adoret te, Deus, et psallat tibi. ℟. Psalmum dicat nomini tuo.
>
> *Ad* Benedictus *antiphona.* O crux benedicta, que sola fuisti digna portare regem
> celorum et Dominum.
>
> *Oratio.* Deus, qui unigeniti filii tui Domini nostri Iesu Christi precioso sanguine
> vivifice crucis vexillum sanctificare voluisti, concede, quesumus, eos qui eiusdem sancte
> crucis gaudent honore, tua quoque ubique protectione gaudere. Per eundem.
>
> *Ad horas antiphone de laudibus, cum capitulis et versiculis ut in inventione [sancte
> crucis], sed in responsoriis non dicitur* alleluia. *Oratio ut supra.*

**Translation**

> *[End of Matins:]* *Te Deum*. *The priest's versicle:* ℣. This sign of the cross shall
> be in the heavens. ℟. When the Lord comes to judge.
>
> **At Lauds.** *Antiphon:* O great work of loving kindness: death then died, when Life
> died upon the tree. *Psalm 92* The Lord hath reigned.
>
> *Antiphon:* Save us, Christ our Saviour, by the power of the holy cross; thou who
> didst save Peter on the sea, have mercy on us. *Psalm 99* Shout with joy.
>
> *Antiphon:* By the sign of the cross deliver us from our enemies, O our God. *Psalm 62*
> O God, my God.
>
> *Antiphon:* The blessed cross shines bright, on which the Lord hung in the flesh, and
> with his blood washed our wounds. *Canticle* Bless [the Lord].
>
> *Antiphon:* Behold the cross of the Lord: flee, ye hostile powers; the lion of the tribe
> of Judah, the root of David, has conquered, alleluia. (cf. Rev 5:5) *Psalm 148* Praise
> the Lord from the heavens.
>
> *Chapter:* But God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus
> Christ, by whom the world is crucified to me, and I to the world. (Gal 6:14) ℟. Thanks
> be to God.
>
> *Hymn:*
> Thou, Christ, most loving King, by the sign of this cross do not refuse to guard us
> every hour and moment;
> That with a voice in harmony with thee and a most devoted heart we may at all times
> render thee the praises that are due.
> Glory to thee, O Lord, who didst save us on the cross, with the Father and the Holy
> Spirit, for ever and ever. Amen.
>
> ℣. Let all the earth adore thee, O God, and sing to thee. ℟. Let it sing a psalm to thy
> name. (Ps 65:4)
>
> *Antiphon at the Benedictus:* O blessed cross, which alone wast worthy to bear the King
> and Lord of heaven.
>
> *Collect:* O God, who willed to hallow the standard of the life-giving cross by the
> precious blood of thine only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ: grant, we beseech
> thee, that those who rejoice in the honour of that same holy cross may rejoice also in
> thy protection everywhere. Through the same [Christ our Lord].
>
> *At the [little] hours, the antiphons of Lauds, with the chapters and versicles as on
> the Finding of the Holy Cross [3 May], but* alleluia *is not said in the responsories.
> The collect as above.*

*Note.* At Lauds the hymn is cued by three opening lines only — *Tu Christe rex
piissime*, *Ut ore tibi consono*, *Gloria tibi Domine*. These are the first lines of the
last three stanzas of the Vespers hymn *Signum crucis mirabile*, printed in full on scan
p. 176, so Lauds sings the second half of that hymn. The printer writes *Tu Christe rex*
at Lauds and *Tu rex Christe* at Vespers.

## Lauds of Our Lady — Saturdays (scan pp. 184–185; collect from p. 181)

**Latin**

> *[End of Matins:]* Te Deum. *Sacerdotalis* ℣. Ora pro nobis, sancta Dei genitrix. ℟.
> Ut digni efficiamur promissionibus Christi.
>
> In laudibus. *Antiphona.* Assumpta est Maria in celum; gaudent angeli, laudantes
> benedicunt Dominum. *Psalmus* Dominus regnavit decorem.
>
> *Antiphona.* Maria virgo assumpta est ad ethereum thalamum, in quo rex regum stellato
> sedet solio. *Psalmus* Iubilate.
>
> *Antiphona.* In odore unguentorum tuorum currimus; adolescentule dilexerunt te nimis.
> *Psalmus* Deus deus meus.
>
> *Antiphona.* Benedicta filia tu a Domino, quia per te fructum vite communicavimus.
> *Psalmus* Benedicite.
>
> *Antiphona.* Pulchra es et decora, filia Hierusalem, terribilis ut castrorum acies
> ordinata. *Psalmus* Laudate Dominum.
>
> *Capitulum.* Ego quasi vitis fructificavi suavitatem odoris, et flores mei fructus
> honoris et honestatis. ℟. Deo gratias.
>
> *Hymnus.* O gloriosa *[cued only]*.
>
> *Ad* Benedictus *antiphona.* Ave, stella matutina, / peccatorum medicina, / mundi
> princeps et regina: / virgo sola digna diceris; / contra tela inimici / clypeum pone
> salutis / tue titulum virtutis. / Tu es enim virga Iesse, / in qua Deus fecit esse /
> Aaron amygdalum, / mundi tollens scandalum. / Tu es area compluta / celesti rore
> imbuta, / sicco tamen vellere. / Tu nos in hoc carcere / solare propicia, / Dei plena
> gratia. / O sponsa Dei electa, / esto nobis via recta / ad eterna gaudia, / ubi pax et
> gloria. / Tu nos semper aure pia, / dulcis, exaudi, Maria.
>
> *Oratio.* Concede nos famulos tuos, quesumus, Domine Deus, perpetua mentis et corporis
> sanitate gaudere, et gloriosa beate Marie semper virginis intercessione a presenti
> liberari tristitia, et eterna perfrui letitia. Per Dominum.
>
> *Ad horas antiphone de laudibus.*

**Translation**

> *[End of Matins:]* *Te Deum*. *The priest's versicle:* ℣. Pray for us, O holy Mother
> of God. ℟. That we may be made worthy of the promises of Christ.
>
> **At Lauds.** *Antiphon:* Mary is taken up into heaven; the angels rejoice, and with
> praises they bless the Lord. *Psalm 92* The Lord hath reigned, [he is clothed with]
> beauty.
>
> *Antiphon:* The Virgin Mary is taken up into the heavenly bridal chamber, where the King
> of kings sits on his starry throne. *Psalm 99* Shout with joy.
>
> *Antiphon:* We run after the fragrance of thy ointments; the maidens have loved thee
> exceedingly. (cf. Song 1:3) *Psalm 62* O God, my God.
>
> *Antiphon:* Blessed art thou, daughter, by the Lord, for through thee we have shared in
> the fruit of life. *Canticle* Bless [the Lord].
>
> *Antiphon:* Thou art beautiful and comely, daughter of Jerusalem, terrible as an army
> set in battle array. (cf. Song 6:3) *Psalm 148* Praise the Lord.
>
> *Chapter:* As the vine I have brought forth a pleasant odour, and my flowers are the
> fruit of honour and riches. (Sir 24:23) ℟. Thanks be to God.
>
> *Hymn:* O glorious [Lady] *[cued only]*.
>
> *Antiphon at the Benedictus:*
> Hail, star of the morning, medicine of sinners, princess and queen of the world: thou
> alone art called worthy. Against the darts of the enemy set up the shield of thy
> salvation, the banner of thy power. For thou art the rod of Jesse, in which God made
> Aaron's almond to be, taking away the world's offence. Thou art the threshing floor
> rained upon, soaked with heavenly dew, while the fleece stayed dry. Comfort us
> graciously in this prison, thou who art full of God's grace. O chosen bride of God, be
> for us the straight road to eternal joys, where there is peace and glory. Always hear
> us with loving ear, O sweet Mary.
>
> *Collect:* Grant, we beseech thee, O Lord God, that we thy servants may rejoice in
> lasting health of mind and body, and, by the glorious intercession of blessed Mary ever
> Virgin, be delivered from present sorrow and come to enjoy eternal gladness. Through
> [our] Lord.
>
> *At the [little] hours, the antiphons of Lauds.*

*Notes.* The Lauds hymn is cued only as *O gloriosa*. This is normally *O gloriosa
domina* (or *femina*), the second half of Venantius Fortunatus's *Quem terra pontus
ethera*; this office's Matins hymn, also only cued here (scan p. 181), is *Quem terra
pontus ethera*. The pages read so far do not print either hymn in full, so no text is
supplied. The Benedictus antiphon *Ave stella matutina* is a rhymed sequence-like chant;
its line breaks above are editorial. The printer's *communicavimus* and *salutis tue
titulum* are kept as printed.

After Lauds and the hours, the book prints a group of Marian antiphons for memorials of
Our Lady (*Alma redemptoris mater*, *Virgo prudentissima*, *Anima mea liquefacta est*,
*Tota pulchra es*, *Descendi in ortum meum*: scan p. 185). They are not specific to Lauds
and are left out here.

# 7. Proper of the Saints (*Sanctorale*) — Lauds

The saints' offices run through the year from the end of November. For each feast only the
elements that bear on Lauds are given: its rank, any proper antiphons, chapter, hymn,
versicle and Benedictus antiphon, the collect, and the rubrics about memorials. Where the
book says a feast is "all from the Common", the Lauds are those of the relevant Common
(see section 8), and only the proper collect is given here.

---

## Opening rubric of the Sanctorale (scan p. 523)

**Latin**

> *Incipiunt officia propria de sanctis per totum annum. Quoniam supra visus est cursus
> temporalis totius anni, modo videnda sunt propria officia sanctorum per totum annum. Et est
> notandum quod omnia festa sanctorum, quecunque sint non habentia proprietatem, recurrendum
> est ad commune sanctorum, prout competit in festivitatibus eorum. Notandum est autem quod
> quotienscunque aliquod festum sanctorum evenerit infra octavas quecunque sint, dicta festa
> transferuntur ad primam diem vacantem post ipsas octavas, nisi expresse preceperit in suis
> locis fieri de ipsis, ut videbitur per ordinem sanctuarii. … Et ultra est notandum quod ab
> octava corporis Christi usque ad adventum, super ebdomadam, diebus vacantibus, feria iii vel
> v, fit de sancto Iohanne baptista, ut visum est post psalterium. … Et de precepto ordinis
> nostri fiendum est, ob reverentiam et honorem patroni nostri, precursoris Domini.*

**Translation**

> Here begin the proper offices of the saints through the whole year. Since the course of
> the season through the whole year has been seen above, now the proper offices of the
> saints through the year are to be seen. Note that for all feasts of saints that have no
> proper texts, recourse is to be had to the Common of the Saints, as fits their feasts. Note
> also that whenever a saint's feast falls within any octave, it is transferred to the first
> free day after the octave, unless it is expressly ordered in its own place that it be kept,
> as will be seen in the order of the Sanctorale. … And further note that from the octave of
> Corpus Christi until Advent, on free weekdays — Tuesday or Thursday — the office is of St
> John the Baptist, as was seen after the psalter. … And this is to be done **by command of our
> Order, out of reverence and honour for our patron, the Forerunner of the Lord.**

*Note.* This confirms the weekday votive office of St John the Baptist ([section
4](04-weekday-votive-offices.md)) as a statute of the Order itself.

## St Saturninus, Bishop and Martyr — 29 November, the vigil of St Andrew (scan p. 523)

> *In vigilia sancti Andree apostoli festum sancti Saturnini fiat ix lectionum, de evangelio
> vigilie sancti Andree …; cetera de dicto sancto, cum oratione sequente.* … *Omnia fiant de
> communi unius martyris.*
>
> *Oratio.* Deus, qui nos beati Saturnini martyris tui concedis natalicio perfrui, eius nos
> tribue meritis adiuvari. Per Dominum.

> On the vigil of St Andrew the feast of St Saturninus is kept with nine lessons, [one
> lesson] from the gospel of the vigil of St Andrew …; the rest of the said saint, with the
> following collect. … *Everything from the Common of one martyr.*
>
> *Collect:* O God, who grantest us to enjoy the heavenly birthday of blessed Saturninus thy
> martyr, grant that we may be helped by his merits. Through [our] Lord.

## St Andrew, Apostle — 30 November (scan p. 528)

**Latin**

> *[End of Matins:] Psalmus* Te Deum. ℣. *sacerdotalis.* Dedisti hereditatem *[timentibus
> nomen tuum, Domine]*.
>
> In laudibus. *Antiphona.* Salve crux speciosa; suscipe discipulum eius qui pependit in te,
> magister meus Christus. *Psalmus* Dominus regnavit.
>
> *Antiphona.* Beatus Andreas orabat dicens: Domine, rex eterne glorie, suscipe me pendentem
> in patibulo. *Psalmus* Iubilate.
>
> *Antiphona.* Andreas, Christi famulus, dignus Dei apostolus, germanus Petri et in passione
> socius. *Psalmus* Deus deus.
>
> *Antiphona.* Maximilla, Christo amabilis, tulit corpus apostoli, optimo loco cum aromatibus
> sepelivit. *Psalmus* Benedicite.
>
> *Antiphona.* Qui persequebantur iustum demersisti eos, Domine, in profundum, et in ligno
> crucis dux iusti fuisti. *Psalmus* Laudate.
>
> *Capitulum* Per manus autem *[apostolorum fiebant signa et prodigia multa in plebe]*.
> *Hymnus* Exultet celum. ℣. Annunciaverunt opera *[Dei]*.
>
> *Ad* Benedictus *antiphona.* Cum pervenisset beatus Andreas ad locum ubi crux parata erat,
> exclamavit et dixit: O bona crux, diu desiderata, et iam concupiscenti animo preparata;
> securus et gaudens venio ad te, ita ut et tu exultans suscipias me discipulum eius qui
> pependit in te.
>
> *Oratio.* Maiestatem tuam, Domine, suppliciter exoramus, ut sicut ecclesie tue beatus
> Andreas apostolus extitit predicator et rector, ita apud te sit pro nobis perpetuus
> intercessor. Per Dominum.
>
> *Ad primam et ad alias horas antiphone de laudibus; cetera ut in communi apostolorum, cum
> oratione supradicta.*

**Translation**

> *[End of Matins:]* The *Te Deum*. *The priest's versicle:* Thou hast given an inheritance
> [to those that fear thy name, O Lord]. (Ps 60:6)
>
> **At Lauds.** *Antiphon:* Hail, beautiful cross; receive the disciple of him who hung on
> thee, my master Christ. *Psalm 92* The Lord hath reigned.
>
> *Antiphon:* Blessed Andrew prayed, saying: O Lord, King of eternal glory, receive me as I
> hang upon the gibbet. *Psalm 99* Shout with joy.
>
> *Antiphon:* Andrew, the servant of Christ, a worthy apostle of God, the brother of Peter and
> his companion in suffering. *Psalm 62* O God, my God.
>
> *Antiphon:* Maximilla, beloved of Christ, took the body of the apostle and buried it with
> spices in the best of places. *Canticle* Bless [the Lord].
>
> *Antiphon:* Those who persecuted the just man thou hast plunged into the deep, O Lord, and on
> the wood of the cross thou wast the leader of the just. *Psalm 148* Praise.
>
> *Chapter* And by the hands [of the apostles many signs and wonders were done among the
> people] (Acts 5:12). *Hymn* Let heaven exult. ℣. They declared the works [of God].
>
> *Antiphon at the Benedictus:* When blessed Andrew came to the place where the cross was
> prepared, he cried out and said: O good cross, long desired and now made ready for my
> longing soul: confident and rejoicing I come to thee, so that thou too mayest joyfully
> receive me, the disciple of him who hung upon thee.
>
> *Collect:* We humbly beseech thy majesty, O Lord, that as blessed Andrew the apostle was a
> preacher and ruler of thy Church, so he may be for us a perpetual intercessor with thee.
> Through [our] Lord.
>
> *At Prime and the other hours, the antiphons of Lauds; the rest as in the Common of
> Apostles, with the collect above.*

*Note.* The antiphons tell the apocryphal *Passion of St Andrew*, which describes his
crucifixion at Patras and his burial by the noblewoman Maximilla.

## St Eligius, Bishop and Confessor — 1 December (scan p. 528)

> *Notandum est quod quando festum sancti Andree celebratur feria ii, dicatur ad secundas
> vesperas capitulum et hymnus de primis vesperis, quia propter dominicam non dicuntur prime
> vespere de sancto, immo de dominica; et tantummodo fit memoria de sancto.*
>
> *Sancti Eligii episcopi et confessoris, ix lectiones; omnia ut in communi unius confessoris
> et episcopi.* … *Oratio* (at the memorial at Vespers): Deus, qui pio pontifici Eligio
> sanandi morbos gentium potestatem tribuisti, presta, quesumus, ut intercessio eius gloriosa
> a vitiis purget nos petentes auxilium, atque membris languentium det salubre remedium. Per.

> *Note that when the feast of St Andrew falls on a Monday, the chapter and hymn of first
> Vespers are said at second Vespers, because on account of the Sunday first Vespers are not
> of the saint but of the Sunday, and only a memorial of the saint is made.*
>
> *St Eligius, bishop and confessor: nine lessons; everything as in the Common of one
> confessor and bishop.* … *Collect:* O God, who didst give the devout bishop Eligius the power
> of healing the diseases of the peoples: grant, we beseech thee, that his glorious
> intercession may cleanse from vice us who seek his help, and give a healing remedy to the
> bodies of the sick. Through [our Lord].

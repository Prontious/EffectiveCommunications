# 2. The psalter — Sunday Lauds (scan pp. 54–55)

In the psalter, Sunday Lauds follows directly after the Sunday nocturns. The **Benedictus
antiphon and the collect** are not given here: they are proper to each Sunday and are
found in the Proper of the Season.

---

## Transition from Matins

**Latin**

> *Sacerdotalis versiculus.* ℣. Excelsus super omnes gentes Dominus. ℟. Et super celos
> gloria eius.

**Translation**

> *The priest's versicle.* ℣. The Lord is high above all nations. ℟. And his glory above
> the heavens. (Ps 112:4)

---

## When the five antiphons are used (rubric)

**Latin**

> *Nota quod laudes* Regnavit Dominus *dicuntur singule super singulos psalmos in prima
> dominica post octavam trinitatis et in ultima ante adventum, in quibus fit de tempore.
> Aliis vero dominicis intermediis una sola antiphona dicatur super quinque psalmos. Et
> idem ordo fiet in dominicis inter octavam epiphanie et septuagesimam.*

**Translation**

> Note that the Lauds [antiphons] *Regnavit Dominus* are sung **one over each psalm** on
> the first Sunday after the octave of Trinity and on the last Sunday before Advent, when
> the office is of the season. On the other Sundays in between, **a single antiphon** is
> said over all five psalms. The same order is kept on the Sundays between the octave of
> Epiphany and Septuagesima.

*Note.* This explains the formula in Rubric 15 (see [section 1](01-rubrics.md)): the date
on which the *Regnavit Dominus* antiphons are "pronounced" is the Sunday on which they
are sung in full, one per psalm — the last Sunday before Advent kept "of the season".

## Psalmody, chapter, hymn and versicle

**Latin**

> In laudibus. *Antiphona.* Regnavit Dominus, precinctus fortitudine, cum decore
> virtutum; cuius sedes parata est in eternum. *Psalmus* Dominus regnavit.
>
> *Antiphona.* Sciamus omnes quia Dominus ipse est Deus, cui iubilemus et exultemus et
> laudemus nomen eius in eternum. *Psalmus* Jubilate.
>
> *Antiphona.* Benedicam te, Domine, in vita mea, ut videam virtutem tuam et gloriam
> tuam. *Psalmus* Deus deus meus.
>
> *Antiphona.* Omnis creatura tua, celi et terre, benedicat Dominum; hymnum dicat et
> superexaltet eum in secula. *Psalmus* Benedicite.
>
> *Antiphona.* Spiritus omnis laudet Dominum, quia ipse dixit et facta sunt omnia;
> mandavit et creata sunt. *Psalmus* Laudate.
>
> *Capitulum.* Benedictio et claritas et sapientia et gratiarum actio, honor, virtus et
> fortitudo Deo nostro in secula seculorum. Amen.

**Translation**

> **At Lauds.** *Antiphon.* The Lord hath reigned, girded with strength, in the beauty of
> his power; his throne is prepared for ever. *Psalm 92* The Lord hath reigned.
>
> *Antiphon.* Let us all know that the Lord himself is God; to him let us shout for joy
> and exult, and praise his name for ever. *Psalm 99* Shout with joy [to God, all the
> earth].
>
> *Antiphon.* I will bless thee, O Lord, all my life long, that I may see thy power and
> thy glory. *Psalm 62* O God, my God [with Psalm 66].
>
> *Antiphon.* Let every creature of thine, of heaven and earth, bless the Lord; let it sing
> a hymn and exalt him above all for ever. *Canticle* Bless [the Lord, all ye works of the
> Lord] (Dan 3).
>
> *Antiphon.* Let every spirit praise the Lord, for he spoke and all things were made; he
> commanded and they were created. *Psalm 148* Praise [the Lord from the heavens], [with
> Psalms 149–150].
>
> *Chapter.* Blessing and glory and wisdom and thanksgiving, honour, power and strength be
> to our God for ever and ever. Amen. (Rev 7:12)

### Hymn after Epiphany — *Eterne rerum conditor* (St Ambrose)

**Latin** (as printed)

> *Post epiphaniam in laudibus. Hymnus.*
>
> Eterne rerum conditor, / noctem diemque qui regis, / et temporum das tempora / ut
> alleves fastidium.
>
> Preco diei iam sonat, / noctis profunde pervigil, / nocturna lux viantibus, / a nocte
> noctem segregans.
>
> Hoc excitatus lucifer / solvit polum caligine; / hoc omnis errorum chorus / viam nocendi
> deserit.
>
> Hoc nauta vires colligit, / pontique mitescunt freta; / hoc ipsa petra ecclesie /
> canendo culpam diluit.
>
> Surgamus ergo strenue: / gallus iacentes excitat, / et somnolentos increpat; / gallus
> negantes arguit.
>
> Gallo canente spes redit, / egris salus refunditur, / mucro latronis conditur, / lapsis
> fides revertitur.
>
> Iesu, labentes respice, / et nos videndo corrige; / si respicis, lapsi stabunt, /
> fletuque culpa solvitur.
>
> Tu lux refulge sensibus, / mentisque somnum discute; / te nostra vox primum sonet, / et
> vota solvamus tibi.
>
> Deo patri sit gloria …
>
> ℣. Dominus regnavit [decorem induit]. *Vel secundum tempus dicitur* ℣. Domine refugium.

**Translation**

> *After Epiphany, at Lauds. Hymn.*
>
> Eternal maker of all things, who rulest night and day, and givest times their seasons
> to lighten our weariness:
>
> Now the herald of the day is calling, watchful through the depth of night — a light by
> night for travellers, marking off one watch of night from the next.
>
> Roused by him, the morning star frees the sky from darkness; at his call the whole crew
> of wanderers abandons its road of harm.
>
> At his call the sailor gathers strength and the sea's straits grow calm; at his call the
> very Rock of the Church [Peter] washed away his fault with weeping.
>
> Let us rise, then, briskly: the cock wakes those lying down, rebukes the drowsy, and
> convicts those who deny.
>
> As the cock crows, hope returns, health flows back to the sick, the robber's dagger is
> sheathed, faith comes back to the fallen.
>
> Jesus, look upon us when we waver, and by your look set us right; if you look on us,
> the fallen will stand, and guilt is dissolved in tears.
>
> Shine, O Light, upon our senses, and shake sleep from our minds; let our voice sound
> first to you, and let us pay our vows to you.
>
> Glory be to God the Father …
>
> ℣. The Lord hath reigned, [he is clothed with beauty]. *Or, according to the season,
> the versicle* Lord, thou hast been our refuge [is said].

### Hymn after Trinity — *Ecce iam noctis*

**Latin** (as printed)

> *Ab octava trinitatis usque ad adventum in laudibus. Hymnus.*
>
> Ecce iam noctis tenuatur umbra, / lucis aurora rutilans coruscat; / nisibus totis
> rogitemus omnes / cunctipotentem,
>
> Ut Deus noster miseratus omnem / pellat languorem, tribuat salutem, / donet et nobis
> pietate patris / regna polorum.
>
> Prestet hoc nobis deitas beata / patris ac nati pariterque sancti / spiritus, cuius
> reboat in omni / gloria mundo. Amen.
>
> ℣. Dominus regnavit.

**Translation**

> *From the octave of Trinity until Advent, at Lauds. Hymn.*
>
> See, the shadow of night is already thinning; the dawn of light glows red and flashes.
> With all our strength let us all entreat the Almighty,
>
> That our God, in his mercy, may drive away all sickness, grant us salvation, and by a
> Father's love give us too the kingdom of heaven.
>
> May the blessed Godhead grant us this — Father, Son and Holy Spirit alike — whose glory
> resounds through all the world. Amen.
>
> ℣. The Lord hath reigned.

*Note.* The printed text gives the Sunday Lauds hymn only for the seasons after Epiphany
and after Trinity; the other seasons have their own hymns in the Proper of the Season.
